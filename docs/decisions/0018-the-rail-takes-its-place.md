# 18. The rail takes its place, and the way back says where it goes

Date: 2026-11-06 · Status: accepted

## Decision

On desktop, an open rail with room beside it takes its place: the page centres in the width right of it. Folded, or on an essay, which has no rail, the page centres on the window. The Time Machine follows the same rule once its rail is built; until then it has no rail and centres on the window. Below the same width as before, the rail still opens over the text.

The path reads "§ Library / name" on desktop, § and the word being one button. Escape, once nothing is open, goes back one place: a page to the library, a settings area to Settings, Settings to the library. It does nothing while words are selected, while a key is held or mid-composition, and only on desktop.

## Why

The rail was chosen on 2026-10-30 never to push the text, so that opening it moved nothing. On a wide screen that left the text off-centre in the space the writer could see, and the library's column, which does centre right of the rail, stood 168px away from the page's. The rail's own reason for being one width, that nothing jumps between screens, was not holding.

§ alone did not say it was the way back. An installed app has no back button to fall back on, and an essay has no rail, so the answer had to live in the path and not in the rail. The word is the destination, not the app's name, which says nothing while you write.

## Consequences

- Folding the rail moves the text by 168px. The scale stays, so the page breaks and page numbers stay.
- The journal's margin rule no longer stands near the rail, so the rail keeps its own edge there, and the journal shows two hairlines.
- The running head keeps clear of the head's controls by the narrower of the two margins, since the page is no longer centred.
- A second Escape after closing a sheet leaves the page. Nothing is lost by it: the words are saved and the place is kept.
