# Elbowpatch

A quiet place to write journals, essays and books, with a memory, for people who want to do the writing themselves.

## Problem
Writing tools are noisy, and they want an account, a monthly fee and now the creative part too. Minimal editors write beautifully but remember little, and version control remembers everything but is ugly for prose. So writers give up one of a page they love, real history, files they own or every device, and lose words along the way.

## For
The maintainer, writing every day on a phone and a desktop, who today uses a mix of a notes app and a plain text editor with no real history. Later: journalers and personal writers, then essayists and novelists.

## Flows
1. Write on the page: open the app, land where you left off, and write with no save button. It worked when the words are there on the next launch, offline or not.
2. Find a piece in the library: a journal, an essay or a book, or start a new one. It worked when one tap opens it at your place.
3. Look back and bring words back: walk through a piece as it was on "Tuesday evening", then bring back a chapter, a day, an essay or one passage. It worked when the old words return and nothing later is lost.
4. Take everything with you: one click for a zip of Markdown with its full history, one piece as Markdown or PDF, and an import that never overwrites. It worked when the writing opens without this app.

## Key screens
- The Page: one column, good type, pages that fill and turn, a running head and a folio. Empty, it is a title and a caret. Serves flow 1.
- The Library: Books, Journal and Essays, as a list or a shelf, each with its word count and a few words from where you left off. Serves flow 2.
- The Time Machine: the whole piece at one moment, a tape of moments under it, and bring back. Serves flow 3.

## Surface
App. A tool people install and come back to every day, on any device, and it works offline.

## V1 and later
- v1: all four flows on phone and desktop. That includes the Time Machine, chapters and Contents, the journal calendar, PDF export, and a plain About & privacy page.
- later, only once the maintainer has written in it daily for 2–3 weeks and it feels good: keep a copy and drafts, sync to one place you connect, search, collections, reordering chapters.

## Constraints
- Never lose a word: the latest text is always safe, you can always go back, and older moments thin out with age. A piece put away can come back for 30 days.
- Works fully offline, with no account, and no writing leaves the device unless the writer connects a place.
- Free forever, open source, and anyone can host their own copy.
- Each piece has its own language, for hyphenation and spellcheck.
- Keyboard, screen reader, enough contrast and reduced motion all work.
- Human language only: moments, drafts, bring this back.

## Identity nouns
Page, shelf, chapter, margin, running head, folio, lamp, elbow patch.

## Out of scope
- AI on the page.
- Subscriptions, a paid tier or an accounts system.
- A proprietary cloud, or any server that holds writing.
- Real-time collaboration.
- Streaks, goals, scores or metrics in the editor.
- Plugins.
- Git words in the interface.
- Matching the big writing apps feature for feature.
