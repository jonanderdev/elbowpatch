# Design

The look of Elbowpatch. Builders follow this file. PROJECT.md §9 still applies: calm, analog, typography first, accessibility required.

## Sources of truth
- **This file** holds the direction and the rules: what the look is, why, and what it never does. It wins on principle.
- **The Figma file** holds the exact screens and components — every screen in paper and night, phone and desktop, built from shared components, colour variables and text styles: [Elbowpatch on Figma](https://www.figma.com/design/yRuD5MDnH3WHbNjqMEUPpB/Elbowpatch) — open to anyone to view. Builders take sizes, spacing, states and copy from there.
- **When they disagree,** the rule here wins until both are put right. A change to the look updates this file (if it changes a rule) and the Figma file (if it changes a screen) together, never one alone.
- The first prototype was retired on 2026-10-27: its screens live in the Figma file, its decisions in this file. Motion was never reviewed in it, so the timings below are proposals (see Motion).

## Direction
- leads: a minimalist e-reader page, in light and dark
- also: a book's chapter opening; focus by dimming at night; one round control that opens into a bar
- from: design exploration, 2026-10-26
- in one line: the calm of a minimalist e-reader — but you are writing it.
- take: The reader's page — a title placed like a chapter opening, a narrow column, generous leading, a page number at the foot. Paging is the model: the page fills, then you turn to the next one; no long scroll. Earlier paragraphs dim at night while the current one stays lit. Nothing else on the page.
- change: It is an editor, not a reader: pages form as you type and a new page begins when one fills, numbered like a book. The turn is a quiet slide, not a 3D paper curl. Reader features (search book, bookmarks, ratings, bylines) are gone; the only status is the page number, and the word count when the tools are open. White, black and grey only for now — no cream, no accent colour; more palettes later.
- ownable: The elbow patch, as the page button — one small round button at the bottom right whose face is the current page number. It is the only raised mark on the page (*Library* is an outline, never a surface) and the only way into the tools; tapped, it opens into the tools bar. The round shape may carry into the logo. No mustache, no pipe, no illustration. (Replaced the earlier oval page marker on 2026-10-26, after seeing it rendered in the prototype.)
- leave: Reader features; a 3D page curl; cream and amber; anything that reads like an existing writing app — monospace text, a signature accent colour.

## Colour

Two themes, both first-class. Paper follows light system appearance, night follows `prefers-color-scheme: dark`. Every pair below was checked with WCAG 2.x contrast.

### Paper
- #f3f3f0: the page — soft paper, a light grey rather than white, so a long session on a lit screen does not glare. (Changed on 2026-10-28 from #fbfbf9, which read as a bright white sheet.)
- #1f1f1f: titles and body text. 14.83:1 (AAA). Also the focus ring (a mark, ≥ 3:1).
- #676767: secondary text (page number, word count, running head). 5.09:1 on the page, 5.66:1 on a surface (AA).
- #ffffff: surfaces — the page button, the tools bar, the settings sheet, the selection menu — lifted by a soft shadow and a 1px hairline edge. Against the grey page they stand clear of it.
- #e2e2de: the hairline edge of a surface and the separator in the tools bar only — never text.
- #c9c9c4: the outline of a word button (see Control) — never text. Decorative: its word already names it.
- #e9e9e5: a pressed or hovered button. Title text on it 13.54:1, secondary 4.65:1.
- #d6d6d0: text selection. Text on it 11.29:1.
- Paper does not dim earlier paragraphs.

### Night
- #0e0e0e: the page — a true black, slightly lifted.
- #e6e6e3: titles, the drop cap, the caret and the focus ring. 15.44:1 (AAA).
- #b5b5b2: body text. 9.39:1 (AAA).
- #8c8c8a: secondary text and dimmed earlier paragraphs. 5.73:1 on the page, 5.17:1 on a surface (AA) — dimming never goes below this.
- #1a1a1a: surfaces (as in paper), lifted by a deeper shadow.
- #2a2a2a: the hairline edge of a surface and the bar separator only.
- #3a3a38: the outline of a word button (as in paper).
- #242424: a pressed or hovered button.
- #3a3a38: text selection. Body on it 5.55:1, titles 9.12:1.

### Not defined yet
- Further palettes (sepia, a warm night) — later, as named themes.

## Type
- **Literata** (TypeTogether, OFL) for titles, body and labels: titles in a light display optical size, body in the text size.
- **EB Garamond italic** (Georg Duffner, OFL) for pull quotes only: a centred paragraph, larger than the text (1.36em, weight 400). Literata's italic is too upright to read as a quote at that size. Chosen on 2026-10-26 over Newsreader italic: it reads as italic at once, holds up on the night page, and gives the one bookish moment on an otherwise modern page.
- Nothing else: no sans, no mono for now.
- Both OFL: bundled in the app, self-hosted, default UI — covered.
- Literata was chosen because it was made for long reading on screens.
- **Body:** Literata at weight 360, 19px on desktop and 18px on a phone, line height 1.55, measure 34rem, old-style figures; paragraphs spaced by one line, no indent. Settings change these within fixed steps (see Control).
- **Title:** weight 300, optical size 60, 2.05 × the body size, line height 1.12, slightly tightened (−0.012em). Title only — no label above it naming the kind of piece (essay, journal) in v1.
- **Running head:** from page 2 on, the piece title in small italic (13px), in secondary grey, like a book's running head: centred over the text column, with the same room on either side as *Library* takes, so it reads balanced rather than pushed by *Library* (changed on 2026-10-28).
- **Drop cap (optional):** the first letter of the piece, four lines deep, weight 600 (SemiBold), optical size 72, title colour, upright. (Three lines read too small, and at weight 500 the hairlines were light beside the text; both changed on 2026-10-27. An EB Garamond italic cap was tried and left: it broke the opening.) Where a browser lacks `initial-letter`, a floated letter of the same depth stands in.
- **Pull quote:** one paragraph, centred, with space above and below; no author line in v1.
- **Indented paragraphs (optional, off by default):** as in a printed book, each paragraph's first line steps in by 1.5em and the space between paragraphs goes. The first paragraph of a piece or chapter, and the one after a pull quote, start flush.
- **Italic in the text:** EB Garamond italic, as the pull quote — scaled to 1.17em so its x-height matches Literata's, with line-height 1 so it sits on the same baseline and never moves the leading. Bold in the text is Literata at 600.
- **Numbers in controls** (page button, word count) are lining and tabular, so they do not jump.
- **Thousands take a dot, European style:** 1.240 words, never 1,240.

## Control
- No filled button on the page, and no text in letter-spaced capitals. Tools appear only when summoned (PROJECT.md §4.1) and on selection, and leave when dismissed.
- **The page button:** a 44px circle at the bottom right, on a surface with a soft lift, showing the current page number (it grows into a pill past page 99). Its label for assistive tech says "Page N of M. Open tools". It shows one number only — the total appears when the tools are open.
- **Showing the way in, once:** a number in a circle can read as a page indicator, not as the way to the tools. So until the tools are opened for the first time on this device, the page shows "Your tools are here" beside the page button — 13px italic, secondary, on the page with no surface, level with the button and ending 10px from it. It stays until that first tap, never goes on a timer, and never shows again after. It hides while the keyboard is up and while another line shows at the foot. Hidden from assistive tech, since the button's label already says "Open tools". (Chosen on 2026-10-28 over changing the button.)
- **The tools bar:** the page button opens into one rounded bar, in place: "3 of 12 · 1.240 words" in secondary grey, then icon buttons — export, Time Machine, text settings (Aa), paper / night (moon or sun), close. The rarest sits furthest from where the bar opens, so the bar's old order stays where the thumb already knows it. Escape or close folds it back into the button. The bar never comes closer than 16px to the screen's edges: when a long count would push it there on a phone, the count drops its last word — "148 of 312 · 41.240" — and its label for assistive tech still says it in full. (Export added on 2026-10-28. Measured: 333px with "1 of 5 · 1.040 words"; 363px with "148 of 312 · 41.240 words", too wide even for a 390px phone; 325px without the word.)
- **Icons:** one set, drawn for this app: 20px, 1.5px stroke, round caps and joins, current text colour — all the same colour, none highlighted. Icon buttons are 36px circles with a soft round pressed tint, no border — every round control is fully round.
- **Text settings:** a sheet over the page, applied live as you change it. Each setting is a segmented control, one row each: size (S / M / L), line spacing (tight 1.42 / normal 1.55 / loose 1.72), alignment (left / justified — icons), theme (Paper / Night, with a small swatch), measure (narrow 34rem / wide 40rem). Drop cap is one on/off switch; Indent paragraphs is another, off by default. "Done" closes it as a text link.
- Justified text always hyphenates.
- **Selection menu:** the same rounded bar, floating over the selected words: bold, italic, pull quote, § (start chapter here), and "keep this moment" (a Time Machine save).
- **Writing on a phone:** above the keyboard, the same bar: undo, redo, a separator, then bold, italic, pull quote, § (new chapter), hide keyboard. A phone has no reliable undo of its own, and moments are up to 5 minutes apart, so undo is on the bar. On desktop it stays ⌘/Ctrl + Z and ⌘/Ctrl + Shift + Z, with nothing on screen.
- **Keyboard up:** the page keeps its size. When the caret would go under the keyboard, the page slides up so the caret line sits just above the writing bar. *Library* and the running head stay, on a strip of page colour with a 44px fade under it (solid for its first 15%), so text leaving the top goes softly instead of meeting them; once the title has slid away, the running head carries it. This is the one fade on the page, only while the keyboard pushes it. Page numbers do not change when the keyboard comes up.
- **Moving around with the keyboard up:** drag the text up or down to see the part of the page behind the keyboard (the caret stays where it is); swipe sideways to turn pages; tap a word to move the caret there. "Hide keyboard" in the writing bar puts the keyboard down; return only ever makes a new paragraph. To go far — the start of the piece, another chapter, another day — tap the page button beside the writing bar: the tools open, and the page count opens Contents (or Days), all without leaving the keyboard.
- **§ is the chapter mark.** Set in Literata like B and I, it starts a new chapter in the writing bar and "starts chapter here" on a selection. Typing `#` at the start of a line does the same.
- **Back to the library:** the word *Library* as a **word button** at the left of the running-head line, its outline on the text's left edge. A word, not an icon: an arrow felt generic and a pile-of-sheets glyph read as "copy". (Provisional: whether it should fade while writing is still open.)
- **The word button:** a word in a hairline pill — 13px in secondary grey, 12px either side, 30px tall inside a 44px-tall tap area, fully round, a 1px outline in the word-button colour, no fill and no shadow. The outline says it can be pressed; the grey keeps it below the title, which leads the page. Hovered or pressed, the word takes the title colour on the pressed tint. The outline, not the word, lines up with its edge: on a phone the text column's (left for *Library*, right for *Contents* / *Days*); on desktop the frame's corners, as the page button's (see Layout). A word lined up instead left the outline hanging in the margin, against nothing. It is for a word that stands alone in the head and takes you somewhere: *Library*, *Settings* (back from a settings area), *Now* in the Time Machine, and *Contents* / *Days* opposite it. Words inside a sentence or at the foot of a sheet (*Undo*, *Export*, *Done*) stay plain words. (Chosen on 2026-10-28: the bare grey word read as a label, not something to press. A surface with a lift was left, so the page button stays the only raised mark. The word in the title colour was tried and left: it outweighed the title.)
- **Focus:** a 2px ring in the title colour, offset 2px, on every control, visible on keyboard focus only.
- **Tooltips:** rare — only where a word's consequence is not visible, as *Put away* (see The library). A small surface, 12px corners, 13px in the title colour, above the control after a short pause on hover or keyboard focus; desktop only. Never the only place the words live: they are the control's description for assistive tech, and on touch the line after the action says them. Icon buttons do not get tooltips for their names.

## Layout
- One page, one narrow column, generous leading. The page carries the design; nothing frames it — no hairline, no fade, no page dots at the foot. (The journal's left margin rule is the one exception; see The journal.)
- Page 1 opens with the piece title like a chapter, then the text. Later pages have the running head instead.
- Margins: 88px above the text and 96px below on desktop; 64px above and 92px below on a phone, 24px each side. With the keyboard up, the page keeps its size and slides up (see Control).
- Top left: *Library*. Bottom right: the page button. That is all the page carries besides the text; the running head sits clear of *Library*.
- **The smallest screen:** 320 × 360, the smallest phones. In a smaller window the screens keep that size and the window scrolls, rather than squeezing the page to nothing. (Added on 2026-10-29.)
- **Where the controls sit:** on a phone, *Library*'s outline is on the text's left edge and the page button 16px from the right. On desktop both go to the corners of the screen, 20px in from both edges, measured to what is drawn (*Library*'s outline, the page button's circle), so each sits square in its corner: *Library* top left, the page button (and the tools bar, the sheets and the lines at the foot) bottom right; *Contents* / *Days* will take the top right. The corners are those of a **frame at most 2560px wide, centred on the screen**: up to an iMac 27" (2560 points) the controls sit at the screen's edges; on anything wider they stay within the frame and never drift further apart. The text column stays centred within it. (Widened on 2026-10-28 from 1280px, which left them floating in from the edges on a large screen.) (Chosen on 2026-10-28: on desktop the text column carries only text, and the two controls frame the screen from opposite corners.)

## The library
- Where the app opens. "Library" set like a piece title; in the top-right corner two quiet icon buttons, in secondary grey: the view switch, then settings.
- **Continue:** on top, under a small heading, *Where you left off* — set like the kind headings (15px italic, secondary grey, 6px above the card) but with no mark — the piece you were last in, as a compact open page — a surface with the soft lift, 16px corners: two lines from where you stopped (slightly smaller than the body, cut with "…"), then one line of *chapter* · book in secondary grey, and the page number in a 28px outlined patch at the right. The meta line never wraps: the book title is cut first, then the chapter. Tapping it opens the piece where you were. (Chosen on 2026-10-27, made compact after a first, taller version.)
- **Grouped by kind:** below it, the pieces under *Books*, *Journal* and *Essays*, each kind named once in small italic secondary grey (15px), most recent first within a kind. Groups breathe: 2.6em above each heading, 0.5em below it, so the gap between kinds is clearly larger than the gap between rows. The piece in Continue is also in its group, so every piece is always found under its kind. (Changed on 2026-10-28: it was left out of its group, which hid a lone piece's place in the list.)
- Each piece is a row: its title (Literata, a little larger than the body) and a line in secondary grey — "6 chapters · 48 pages · 9 Oct" for a book, "214 days · nothing yet today" for the journal, "1.240 words · 25 Oct" for an essay. Rows are separated by space, not rules, with a soft rounded pressed tint.
- **Kinds have marks, not drawings:** typographic marks set in Literata, regular, as § is for a chapter — **&** for a book (and so on, chapter after chapter), **today's date with its ordinal** for the journal ("27th": the ordinal half size and raised, since Literata has no superior letters; it changes daily), **¶** (the pilcrow) for an essay. 19px in the new-piece bar (title colour, as its word), 19px at the foot of a start object in the empty library (secondary grey). The library's group headings are the word alone: under *Books* the rows already say what they are, and a mark there only crowded the list. (Chosen on 2026-10-27 from 36 marks Literata draws itself, over line drawings, which felt busier than §; taken out of the group headings on 2026-10-28.)
- **Two views, list or shelf:** the list (the rows above) is the default. The view switch shows the view it leads to — a list icon (three lines) in the shelf, a shelf icon (two upright books and a third leaning back against them) in the list. The choice is remembered. An empty library has no switch, only settings. (Added on 2026-10-27 as an option, not a replacement.)
- **The shelf:** each kind's pieces in one row that scrolls sideways, under the same headings. The row runs to the right edge of the screen so the next piece shows cut off there — the only sign that it scrolls; no arrows, no dots. Each piece is an outlined object, 100 × 140, 12px apart: a 1px line in secondary grey, filled with the page colour, no shadow and no surface — so it never competes with Continue. Its shape says its kind: a **book** has a spine line near its left edge and squarer corners on that side; a **journal** has rounded corners and a band down the right; an **essay** is a sheet with its top-right corner folded. The title sits inside at the top (15px, title colour, at most three lines, then cut with "…"); at the foot, in 12px secondary grey, at most two short lines: "3 chapters" and "21 pages" for a book, "214 days" for a journal, "1.240 words" for an essay. Dates are left to the list.
- **The shelf on desktop wraps:** the rows stay inside the library column — five pieces fill it, lined up with the edges of Continue — and a kind with more starts a new row below, 16px apart. Nothing scrolls sideways and nothing is cut at the edge, since sideways scrolling is awkward with a mouse wheel and the column has room for a full row. (Chosen on 2026-10-27 over rows running out to the window edge, with or without a fade past the column.)
- **The foot is not reserved:** the library runs to the bottom of the screen and the new-piece button floats over it, so the last row is never cut early to keep space free.
- **New piece:** the round button at the bottom right, in the place of the page button, with a +. Tapped, it opens into a bar like the tools bar: Book, Journal, Essay — each its icon and its word — then a separator and close, where the + was. Choosing one opens its first page. A new piece left empty is not kept.
- **The piece menu:** press and hold a piece on touch, or right-click it, or focus it and press the menu key or Shift + F10: the rounded bar of the selection menu floats over that piece — *Rename*, *Export*, *Put away*, then a separator and close — and the piece takes the pressed tint. The same in the list and on the shelf. *Rename* edits the title where it stands. *Export* opens the export sheet (see Export). *Put away* hides it at once, without asking — nothing is lost — and one calm line shows: "Put away for 30 days. *Undo*", *Undo* in the title colour, for about 6 seconds (a little longer than other lines, to reach it); ⌘/Ctrl + Z undoes it too while the line shows. On desktop, hovering or focusing *Put away* shows a tooltip: "Hidden for 30 days. Bring it back from Settings." The same words are its description for assistive tech. (Chosen on 2026-10-28; also answers what pressing and holding a piece does. Undo was chosen over a confirmation, which would be tapped through.)
- **A line in the library** — after putting away, after exporting from the piece menu — sits on a small rounded bar at the foot, left of the new-piece button: a surface with the soft lift, 44px tall, 16px in from the edge. The library's foot is not reserved, so a bare line would sit over a row.
- The library fades in over the page (220ms; instant with reduced motion). The page behind is inert while the library shows.
- **Empty:** centred in the space below the title, the § mark large (Literata Light, 96px, secondary grey), then one calm line, "Begin anywhere." — the same words an empty chapter shows, so both empty moments speak alike — then a row of three **start objects**: the shelf shapes drawn with a dashed line — *Start a book*, *Start a journal*, *Start an essay* inside, the kind's mark at the foot. Tapping one starts that kind, as the new-piece bar does; the dashes say it is not written yet. No view switch, no Continue. (Chosen on 2026-10-28 over an illustration, which the Imagery rules leave out; the § and the start shelf use only what the app already draws.)
- **No warning on first launch.** Most browsers keep the app's storage once asked (0010), so the library does not open on a risk. Two quiet things instead:
  - **The export reminder:** only after 30 days without a copy (counted from the oldest piece if never exported), one line at the foot of the library, left-aligned under the last group with the new-piece button's height free below it, 13px secondary: "Last exported 5 weeks ago · *Export*" ("Not exported yet · *Export*" before the first), *Export* in the title colour. *Export* downloads everything at once, as Settings › Export & import does, and the line goes until the next 30 days. Once a place to keep a copy can be connected (Backup & sync), the line also offers "· *Connect a place*", and a connected place ends the reminder. (Chosen on 2026-10-28 over a storage note for everyone on first run, which overstated a risk most browsers do not have.)
  - **Safari outside the Home Screen**, the one real risk, is said once: in the empty library under the start objects, and later at the library's foot: "Safari clears this after 7 days away. Add Elbowpatch to your Home Screen." with *Not now*. It never covers the start objects and never blocks the first tap. "Provided as-is" is said in About & privacy, not here.
- Main settings (account, global options, connectors) open from the library only, never from the page.

## Books and chapters
- A book is one piece made of chapters. A chapter starts on a fresh page with its title set like page 1's, and no running head on that page — as a printed book does. Later pages carry the chapter title as their running head.
- **The title page:** a book opens on its own title page, as a printed book does: the title centred across the page in the upper third (the optical centre, not the dead centre, where a block looks to sink), clearly larger than a piece title (3 × the body, light), so it stands well apart from the subtitle, and under it an optional **subtitle** in Literata italic, 1.15 × the body, in the text colour. Empty, the title shows "Untitled" and the subtitle "Subtitle", both in secondary grey and always, so it is clear both can be written in. (Changed on 2026-10-28 from showing "Subtitle" only while on the title page: hidden, nobody found it.) The placeholder lies behind the empty line, so the caret stands centred, where the words will start, never at the line's left edge. No author line (no bylines). Chapter 1 begins on page 2. A new book opens with the caret in its title; Return goes on to the subtitle, then chapter 1's title, then the text. No running head on the title page. The subtitle is kept in the outline with the title, so the Time Machine remembers it. (Chosen on 2026-10-28: the book's name had no place on the page, and could only be set from the library; centred with a subtitle on 2026-10-28, after real books' title pages; enlarged from 2.4 × on 2026-10-28, when the title and subtitle read too alike.)
- A new chapter's title shows "Chapter title" in secondary grey until written, then "Begin anywhere."
- The page button keeps counting the whole book.
- **Contents:** tapping "18 of 48" in the tools bar opens a sheet like text settings: "Contents" and the book's page count, then each chapter with the page it starts on (right, tabular, secondary). The current chapter has the soft pressed tint. "§ New chapter" and "Done" are text links at its foot. Contents is a sheet, never a page in the book, so page numbers do not shift while writing; on export a contents page is printed at the front.

## The journal
- A journal is one piece; each day begins on a fresh page with the date as its title ("Monday 26 October"). The date is not editable — an entry with its own title is an essay.
- **The title page:** a journal opens on a title page as a book does, before its first day: its own name ("Journal", "Dream journal") and an optional subtitle, centred in the upper third, set as a book's. A new journal is called "Journal", selected, so typing names it; Return goes on to the subtitle, then to the first day's text. The name can also be changed from the library. (Changed on 2026-10-28: the name could only be set from the library, so a new journal seemed to have no title.)
- Opening the journal lands on today. Turning back goes to earlier days. A long day runs onto the next page, with the date as the running head.
- **The margin:** the journal alone has a hairline (the surface-line colour) down the left margin, from top to bottom. On a phone it sits 24px from the edge and the text starts at 44px; on desktop it sits 44px left of the text. A deliberate exception to "nothing frames the page", for the journal only.
- **The day's words** sit at the foot, upright, 12px secondary: "312 words" at the text's left edge, level with the page button on the right. Not rotated, not beside the title. (Chosen on 2026-10-27 over a count beside the title, which sat at no clear line.)
- **The way in:** "26 Oct · 312 words" in the tools bar opens a Days sheet as a **month** (in the MVP; changed on 2026-10-27 from a list): the month and year between two arrow icon buttons for the months before and after, a seven-column grid under one-letter weekday initials (12px, secondary), each day a round button — written days in the title colour at weight 560, today with the pressed tint and a hairline ring, days still to come disabled. At the foot, "19 days written in October" (13px, secondary) and "Done". Each day is labelled for assistive tech ("26 October, written").
- The same sheet is also drawn as a list in the Figma file, as an alternative not in the MVP: "Days" and how many are written, the month in italic, then each written day with its word count, today first ("nothing yet" until begun).

## The Time Machine
Designed on 2026-10-27. How it opens and what one version holds are in docs/decisions/0008 and 0005.
- **One moment, the whole piece:** the Time Machine stands at a point in time and shows the whole piece as it was then. Turning pages crosses chapters (or days) as reading does; a chapter that did not exist then is not there. The tape steps through the moments when the section on screen changed, so every step shows a difference; turning into another section keeps the same time and shows that section as it was then.
- **The way in:** the Time Machine icon in the tools bar, or ⌘/Ctrl + Alt + Z (a step further back than undo). It takes the place of the page and opens on the most recent moment that differs from now, so the first thing seen is already the past.
- **The past reads by its frame, never by its text:** the old text keeps full contrast — no tint, no grey, no caret, and night does not dim earlier paragraphs. *Library* becomes *Now* (same place, same style), which goes back to the present, as does Escape. The page button is gone; the moment bar takes the foot.
- **The head:** the running head does its usual job — the chapter title in a book, the date in a journal, the essay's title after page 1. At the top right, mirroring *Now*: *Contents* in a book, *Days* in a journal, which open that sheet **as it was at that moment**, above the moment bar. An essay has nothing there. Navigation sits at the top; what you can do with the moment sits at the foot.
- **The moment bar:** one rounded surface, like the tools bar. Full width less 16px each side on a phone; centred at the column's width on desktop. Three parts, top to bottom:
  - **The moment**, named in plain words, Literata italic 15px, title colour: "Sunday evening".
  - **The tape:** one 1px tick per moment, evenly spaced (about 11px; up to 44px when there are few, so the tape spreads across the bar), sliding under a fixed 1.5px hairline in the title colour that marks this moment. The right end is now; the tape is as long as the history, and older moments come in from the left. Kept moments ("keep this moment") are ticks in the title colour; the rest are secondary. Labels (12px, secondary) sit under taller ticks and change unit with age, as thinning does: hours today ("9:00", "13:00", "Now"), weekdays within the week ("Sat", "Mon"), dates within the year ("16 Oct"), months before ("Sep"). A label that would meet its neighbour is left out, as on a ruler. The tape fades at its edges to show it goes on; labels never fade.
  - **The actions:** two equal halves, 44px tall, split by the bar separator: *Bring … back* and *Keep a copy*, in the title colour.
  - The tape is dragged or swiped itself, glides and settles on a tick; a tap on a tick goes to it. No arrows.
- **Bring … back** names what it replaces: *Bring chapter back* in a book, *Bring day back* in a journal, *Bring essay back* in an essay. Nothing else in the piece changes. It does not ask first, because nothing is lost: it closes onto the page with the old text in place, and a calm line shows at the foot for a few seconds (two lines on a phone) — "Chapter 3 brought back from Sunday evening. The rest of the book is as it was." The text it replaced stays a moment, never thinned.
- **Removed and renamed chapters:** Contents as it was lists the chapters of that moment under their titles of that moment; a chapter removed since carries "removed since" in secondary where its page number would be. It opens like any other, and *Bring chapter back* puts it back in its old place — or at the end, if the chapters around it are gone — with its old title. Contents as it was has no "§ New chapter": the past is read-only.
- **Bringing back a part:** the old text is read-only but selectable. A selection shows a bar with two words: *Bring part back* and *Copy*. Bringing a part back returns to now and inserts the passage where it stood — after the paragraph that came before it, found as a reading place is (paragraph, offset, a few words), or at the end of the section if that paragraph is gone. It arrives as its own paragraph, left selected so it can be moved or trimmed, and one undo removes it. It never replaces or merges anything. Its line: "Brought back from Sunday evening."
- **Keep a copy** copies the section only and opens the copy at once as a new essay ("Chapter 3, draft 2"), with one line: "A copy of Sunday evening, kept as a new essay." The piece it came from is untouched. Copying a whole book as it was is after the MVP.
- **Nothing earlier:** the bar holds one line, "Nothing earlier yet. Moments are kept as you write." — no tape, no actions.
- **Dates in plain words:** "Just now" under a minute; "12 minutes ago" within the hour; "This afternoon, 14:05" today; "Yesterday evening, 21:10"; "Sunday evening" within the week; "14 October" this year; "14 October 2025" before. A 24-hour clock. Morning 5–12, afternoon 12–18, evening 18–23, night 23–5.
- **Moving around:** sideways swipe on the page, Page Up / Page Down, Alt + ← / → and the side margins still turn pages of the old text; swipes on the tape move the tape. The tape is a slider: ← / → step a moment when it has focus, Home goes to the oldest, End to now.
- **For assistive tech:** the tape is a slider whose value is the full date ("Sunday 25 October, 21:10"); a change of moment is announced, as a page turn is.

## Settings
- Opened from the library only. The same view as the library: "Settings" set like a piece title, *Library* at the top left to go back. Each area is a row that says what it is set to: Writing ("Medium · normal spacing · narrow · left"), Appearance, Time Machine ("Thinned with age · 14 MB"), Backup & sync ("GitHub · last copy 2 minutes ago"), Export & import ("Last exported 12 October", or "Never exported yet"), Put away ("2 pieces · the next goes in 4 days", or "Nothing put away"), About & privacy.
- Each area is its own view, its name as the title and *Settings* at the top left to go back. On desktop, the areas are words on the left (the open one with the pressed tint) and the open area on the right.
- **Writing:** defaults for new pieces, with the text settings' segmented controls and the drop cap switch. Each piece can still change its own in Aa.
- **Backup & sync:** one sentence on where the writing lives. **One place at a time:** the connected place is a surface at the top — its name, "Copying here · last copy 2 minutes ago · as you write" and "Copy now". Other places sit below under *Use another place instead*, each with "Switch" (or "Not available yet"). Switching asks once in a sheet — "Copy to Dropbox instead? GitHub stops receiving copies. Everything already there stays" — with "Keep GitHub" and "Switch to Dropbox". "Disconnect" is a text link in the note under the list. Every action is a word; no filled button. No Git words, apart from the provider's own name.
- Nothing connected: the sentence says plainly that the writing lives only in this browser, and that clearing it loses everything; "Connect" sits beside GitHub.
- **Time Machine:** nothing to set in v1. One sentence on how moments are kept — "While you write, a moment is kept when you pause, at most one every 5 minutes, and always when you leave the page. After an hour, one an hour is kept for a day, then one a day for a month, one a week for a year, and one a month after that. Moments you keep are never thinned." — and the space they use. No "keep everything" switch: it would be the one setting that fills the browser's storage.
- **Export & import:** built like Backup & sync — one sentence, then two rows like its places, each a name, a status line and its word at the right; a note under them. (Chosen on 2026-10-28 over a sentence and a word per part.)
  - The sentence: "Everything you have written, as plain .md files any app can open, with every moment kept and the pieces put away."
  - **Everything** — "14 MB · last exported 12 October" — *Export*. It downloads at once, without asking — nothing is lost by exporting — as `Elbowpatch 28 October 2026.zip`, and the status line becomes "Everything is in Elbowpatch 28 October 2026.zip."
  - **Files** — "An Elbowpatch export, or loose .md files. Nothing here is ever replaced." — *Import*, which opens the file picker (`.zip` and `.md`, several at once). Our own export comes in as it was, kinds and history included, with no question. Loose files open the import sheet first.
  - The note, in secondary: "To export one piece, use the export button on its page, or press and hold it in the library."
  - **The import sheet:** "Import" and how many files, then one row per file — its title, and a segmented control *Book* / *Journal* / *Essay* with a guess already chosen: two or more `# ` headings guess a book (each heading becomes a chapter); headings that all read as dates guess a journal (each date becomes a day, always in a **new** journal, never merged into one that exists); anything else, an essay. *Journal* is offered only when the headings are dates. *Import* and *Cancel* are text links at its foot. (Chosen on 2026-10-28 over guessing silently.)
  - The result takes the place of the Files status line: "3 pieces added." — with "2 were already here." when identical copies were skipped. A long import shows "Adding 120 of 340…", never a progress bar. A newer format: "This export is from a newer Elbowpatch. Update the app and try again. Nothing was added."
  - The word is *Import*, never "Bring in", which is too close to *Bring … back*.
- **Put away:** where put-away pieces wait (docs/decisions/0006). One sentence — "Pieces you put away wait here for 30 days, then go for good. Bringing one back returns it to the library as it was, with all its moments." — then a row per piece, like a place in Backup & sync: its title, "Essay · put away 23 October · 25 days left" (in its last week, "goes in 4 days"), and *Bring back* at the right. Bringing one back needs no question; it goes to its kind in the library, and one calm line shows: "Brought back to the library." The note under the rows: "Every full export includes what is put away, so a copy outlives the 30 days." Nothing there: "Nothing is put away." (Designed on 2026-10-28.)

## Export
Designed on 2026-10-28. What the files hold is in docs/decisions/0007.
- **Two ways in, one sheet:** the export icon in the tools bar, and *Export* in the piece menu in the library. The icon is a sheet with a short arrow coming down out of it — taking a copy out — never a box with an arrow going up, which promises sharing.
- **The export sheet:** a sheet like text settings: "Export", with the piece's title at the right in secondary, as Contents shows its page count, then two rows, each a word in the title colour with one line under it in secondary:
  - *Markdown* — "One .md file, plain text any app can open." It downloads at once as `<Title>.md`: the whole piece in one file, a book with a `# ` heading per chapter, a journal with a `# ` heading per day that carries the year (`# Monday 26 October 2026`, though the page shows the date without it) so the file can be read back across years; no history. The sheet closes, and one calm line shows at the foot of the screen for a few seconds — on the page as the *Bring … back* line does, in the library on its small bar (see The library): "Saved as The Long Winter.md."
  - *PDF* — "Numbered pages to print or keep, with contents at the front." for a book; "…, each day on a fresh page." for a journal; "Numbered pages to print or keep." for an essay. It opens the browser's print dialog.
  - "Done" as a text link at its foot.
- **The printed page reads like the piece, not the app:** always paper colours, whatever the theme. The paper size is the print dialog's. The margins are set by the text, not the paper: the column keeps its reading measure — about 65 characters, 117mm at 11pt — centred on the sheet, so the margins are what is left: about 46mm at the sides on A4, 49mm on Letter, never less than 20mm; 25mm above, and 30mm below, where the page number sits. Type in points: body Literata 11 with the screen's leading (1.55) and paragraph space; titles 2.05 × the body, light, as on screen; the running head 8.5 italic, centred in the top margin; the page number 9, centred in the foot margin. Page 1 opens with the title as on screen, with the drop cap and indented paragraphs if the piece uses them; the running head carries from page 2; each chapter and each day starts on a fresh page, as on screen. The page number in secondary grey, lining figures — the one place a page number leaves its button. A book opens with a contents page that is neither numbered nor counted, so the first chapter starts on page 1, as in a printed book: the book's title set as a title, then *Contents* in small italic secondary, then each chapter with its printed page number (right, tabular, secondary). A journal has no contents page; its margin rule prints, 12mm left of the text, and its page number sits flush with the text's right edge instead of centred — the rule holds the left, the number the right, as the page button does on screen; centred, it floated against the rule. (Chosen on 2026-10-28 from pages drawn on A4 and Letter in the Figma file.) Nothing of the app is printed: no *Library*, no page button, no mark.
- **When a save fails:** "Can't save right now. Your words are still here." and *Save a copy* — one calm line at the foot of the page, where the *Bring … back* line shows, in the same style. Unlike that line it stays until a save succeeds, then goes. *Save a copy* downloads `<Title>.md`, the same file as *Markdown*. The line is announced to screen readers.

## Patterns
- Patterns to follow: settings as a quiet sheet over the page, applied live; a single round control that opens into a bar; themes named for a mood, not a hex; writing tools on the selection, not in a toolbar; a calm one-line serif sentence for empty and closing states.

## Behaviour
- **Paging, not scrolling.** Writing fills a page; the next page begins when it is full and the view turns to it. Page breaks re-flow as text changes.
- **Turning a page** (decided 2026-10-27):
  - **Phone:** swipe sideways — left for the next page, right for the one before. No tap zones at the edges: a tap in the text places the caret, and the 24px side margin is too narrow to tell the two apart. A vertical drag is kept for seeing what is behind the keyboard (see Control).
  - **Desktop:** Page Up / Page Down or Alt + ← / →, and a click in the wide side margins turns back (left) or forward (right), but only farther than 80px from the text, where no click is aimed at a word, and never at the end of a drag, which is selecting. There the pointer becomes an arrow drawn as our icons are (← or →, 1.5 strokes, round ends, in the title colour with a page-coloured edge), only where there is a page to go to, and it holds while the button is down, so clicking through many pages never flickers it. The margins are for turning: on the first or last page a click there does nothing — it never reaches the text, and the caret stays where it was. (The band beside the text added on 2026-10-28; the arrows, and margin clicks kept from the text, on 2026-10-28.)
  - **Scrolling does not turn pages,** with the wheel or the trackpad, up, down or sideways. The page is not a scroll, and a scroll that turned one page and no more was neither paging nor scrolling, only confusing; a sideways swipe is also the browser's Back, so on the page it does nothing at all. (Decided on 2026-10-28, over one page per gesture.)
  - Each turn is the same quiet slide, and is announced to screen readers.
  - **A turn takes the caret along**, to the end of the text on the page turned to, forward or back: that is where words are added, almost never in between, so typing goes straight on without a click. While writing always; on desktop also while reading, so a turn always leaves the page ready to type (not when the tools or a sheet have the focus). On a phone a swipe while reading does not raise the keyboard: a tap places the caret. Where the page ends mid-paragraph the caret sits just before its last character (mostly the space after the last word): the position after it is also the next page's first, and a browser may paint the caret there or at the page's top. On the last page that is the end of the piece. A book's title page, which holds no text, takes it to the end of the book's title. At the first or last page nothing turns, and the caret stays put. (Decided on 2026-10-28, after the caret was left floating where the title is; the end of the page, over its first letter, the same day.)
  - **↑ and ↓ move a line at a time, keeping their place across;** aiming past a shorter line's end, the caret stops after its last word (not after the space where the line wraps, which a browser can paint on the next line). On a page's last line ↓ goes to the next page's first line; on its first line ↑ goes back to the last line of the page before. On the text's first line ↑ goes to its first letter, and on its last line ↓ to its last letter, as in any text field.
  - **← and → cross a page break in one press:** from a page's last letter to the next page's first, and back. Where a page breaks mid-paragraph, the spot after the earlier page's last space is also the next page's first, and a browser can paint the caret there at the page's top (Firefox does): the caret never stops on it, but goes a letter on, the way it was going. (Decided on 2026-10-28, from a recording of the caret painted beside a chapter's title.)
- **A page is what fits the screen** at the current text settings — not a fixed book page. Larger text makes more pages, never a scroll, so nothing ever slides under the head or the foot and no fade is needed. Chosen on 2026-10-27 over a fixed book page, which on a phone means scrolling inside every page and text cut at the edges.
- **Page numbers are for orientation, not addresses.** They follow the screen, as on an e-reader: page 18 on a phone is not page 18 on a desktop. Finding things is by chapter (Contents) and by date (journal), never by page number. Fixed, printable numbers come only from the export.
- **Your place travels with the piece.** The app keeps where you were: the caret if you were writing, otherwise the first line on screen — as a paragraph, an offset and a few words of its text, so it survives edits made elsewhere (if the words are gone, the nearest paragraph). With a connected place this goes with the piece, so another device opens on the page that holds it, caret restored. The most recent position wins; it is only a position, nothing is lost. **Coming back** to a piece left while writing — from the library, or on the next launch — puts the caret where it was, ready to type, on desktop. On a phone the piece opens on that page with the caret placed, and the keyboard waits for a tap: raised unasked, it would cover half the page. A piece left while reading opens on that page, with no caret. (Decided on 2026-10-28.)
- **The turn** is a quiet horizontal slide (timing in Motion). With `prefers-reduced-motion` it is an instant cut. A page change is announced to screen readers; typing is not.
- **Night focus:** paragraphs before the current one dim to #8c8c8a; the current paragraph stays at body colour. Never dimmer than 4.5:1. Paper does not dim.
- **The tools bar** opens out of the page button and folds back into it.
- Settings and the theme are remembered; until the writer picks, the theme follows the system.

## Motion
Proposed, **not yet reviewed** — carried over from the first prototype, where motion was never checked. Decide each before it is built; these are starting points, not rules.
- What moves: the page turn (a horizontal slide), the tools bar opening out of the page button and folding back, the library fading in over the page, sheets (text settings, Contents, Days, confirmations) appearing over the page, the night dimming as the caret moves between paragraphs, the theme changing, the page sliding up when the keyboard comes, and the Time Machine opening and changing moment.
- Starting values: the turn 460ms ease-out; the tools bar opening 340ms from the 44px circle; the library fade 220ms; colour changes (theme, night dimming) 300–400ms; presses 160ms; the Time Machine opening and closing as a 220ms cross-fade; a change of moment a 120ms fade in place — never a slide, so it never reads as a page turn; the tape follows the finger, then glides and settles on the nearest tick.
- Fixed already: one quiet kind of motion, never a 3D paper curl; with `prefers-reduced-motion` every move is an instant cut; nothing moves while you type except the page turning when it fills.

## Gaps
Open — treat as undesigned, not as solved by a familiar pattern:
- The writing state on a real phone: designed (keyboard up, writing bar), but never tested with a real keyboard, or with editing in the middle of a long piece.
- Motion: proposed, not reviewed (see Motion).
- The Time Machine: designed (see The Time Machine), but the tape has not been tried in the hand — its glide and settle, and a history of a year; whether a passage brought back lands in the right place after heavy edits needs a test once the page is built.
- Settings areas not yet drawn: Appearance, About & privacy.
- Reordering chapters and Collections (folders) are after the MVP.
- Printed page numbers: a browser's own print cannot number the pages of a contents page, and not every browser prints a number at the foot from a stylesheet. The printed page may need paging by the app before printing. Try it in Safari and Chrome before the PDF is promised as designed.
- A photo placed inside a piece.
- Installing: an invitation to add the app to the Home Screen (or install it), and an area for it in Settings. What it must say is in docs/decisions/0010; how it looks is not designed.

Paged writing is new ground: typing on a paged page, a phone keyboard, Markdown, an empty first page and a long title being written all still need designing.

## Do not
- Scroll a long page endlessly; page it.
- Fade over readable text, or add a hairline, fade or dots at the foot of the page. (The one exception: the short fade under the head while the keyboard pushes the page up.)
- Use a 3D paper curl, page textures, parchment, typewriter fonts, quills, or any old-writer illustration in the interface.
- Add an accent colour, cream, or amber in this pass.
- Add a third font family without a reason recorded here.
- Label the kind of piece above the title, or credit a pull quote, in v1.
- Put a filled button on the page, or reader features (search book, bookmarks, ratings, bylines).
- Use any text colour below 4.5:1 on its page.
- Use Git words in the UI (PROJECT.md §3): say moments, drafts, bring this back.
- Borrow another product's name, typefaces or colours.
