# Design

The look of Elbowpatch. Builders follow this file. PROJECT.md §9 still applies: calm, analog, typography first, accessibility required.

## Sources of truth
- **This file** holds the direction and the rules: what the look is, why, and what it never does. It wins on principle.
- **The Figma file** holds the exact screens and components — every screen in paper and night, phone and desktop, built from shared components, colour variables and text styles: [Elbowpatch on Figma](https://www.figma.com/design/yRuD5MDnH3WHbNjqMEUPpB/Elbowpatch) — open to anyone to view. Builders take sizes, spacing, states and copy from there.
- **When they disagree,** the rule here wins until both are put right. A change to the look updates this file (if it changes a rule) and the Figma file (if it changes a screen) together, never one alone.
- The first prototype was retired on 2026-10-27: its screens live in the Figma file, its decisions in this file. Motion was never reviewed in it, so the timings below are proposals (see Motion).
- The Figma file's **Explorations** page holds work that is not part of the design: the layout explorations behind the desktop rail (2026-10-30), the head proposal behind One head on every screen (2026-10-30, now applied to the screens), and highlighting text, a proof of concept that is not planned. Nothing there is a rule.

## Direction
- leads: a minimalist e-reader page, in light and dark
- also: a book's chapter opening; focus by dimming at night; one round control that opens into a bar
- from: design exploration, 2026-10-26
- in one line: the calm of a minimalist e-reader — but you are writing it.
- take: The reader's page — a title placed like a chapter opening, a narrow column, generous leading, a page number at the foot. Paging is the model: the page fills, then you turn to the next one; no long scroll. Earlier paragraphs dim at night while the current one stays lit. Nothing else on the page.
- change: It is an editor, not a reader: pages form as you type and a new page begins when one fills, numbered like a book. The turn is a quiet slide, not a 3D paper curl. Reader features (search book, bookmarks, ratings, bylines) are gone; the only status is the page number at the foot, and the word count with the tools (always in the head on desktop, when the tools are open on a phone). White, black and grey only for now — no cream, no accent colour; more palettes later.
- ownable: The elbow patch, as the page button on a phone — one small round button at the bottom right, under the thumb, whose face is an asterisk — the footnote mark, "there is more here". It is the only raised mark on the page and the way into the tools; tapped, it opens into the tools bar. The round shape may carry into the logo. No mustache, no pipe, no illustration. (Replaced the earlier oval page marker on 2026-10-26, after seeing it rendered in the prototype. Its face was the page number until 2026-10-30: a number in a circle read as a page indicator, not an action, so the number moved to the foot of the page as a folio, and the face became ⋯, then on the same day Literata's asterisk: ⋯ is every app's "more"; the asterisk says the same in the language of books, beside § and ¶. On desktop the button is gone: the tools sit in the head, where a wide screen has room for them — see Layout.)
- leave: Reader features; a 3D page curl; cream and amber; anything that reads like an existing writing app — monospace text, a signature accent colour.

## Colour

Two themes, both first-class. Paper follows light system appearance, night follows `prefers-color-scheme: dark`. Every pair below was checked with WCAG 2.x contrast.

### Paper
- #f3f3f0: the page — soft paper, a light grey rather than white, so a long session on a lit screen does not glare. (Changed on 2026-10-28 from #fbfbf9, which read as a bright white sheet.)
- #1f1f1f: titles and body text. 14.83:1 (AAA). Also the focus ring (a mark, ≥ 3:1).
- #676767: secondary text (the folio, word count, running head) and icons at rest (see Control, Icons). 5.09:1 on the page, 5.41:1 on a surface (AA).
- #fafaf8: surfaces — the page button, *Write* on a phone, the tools bar, the settings sheet, the selection menu — lifted by a soft shadow and a 1px hairline edge, which are what set them apart: the colour is only a whisper above the page (1.06:1). Titles on a surface 15.77:1. (Changed on 2026-10-30 from #ffffff, which glowed against the grey page and pulled the eye to the page button.)
- #e2e2de: the hairline edge of a surface and the separator in the tools bar only — never text.
- #e9e9e5: a pressed or hovered button. Title text on it 13.54:1, secondary 4.65:1.
- #d6d6d0: text selection. Text on it 11.29:1.
- Paper does not dim earlier paragraphs.

### Night
- #0e0e0e: the page — a true black, slightly lifted.
- #e6e6e3: titles, the drop cap, the caret and the focus ring. 15.44:1 (AAA).
- #b5b5b2: body text. 9.39:1 (AAA).
- #8c8c8a: secondary text and dimmed earlier paragraphs. 5.73:1 on the page, 4.95:1 on a surface (AA) — dimming never goes below this.
- #1e1e1e: surfaces (as in paper), lifted by a deeper shadow; one step clearer of the page than paper's (1.16:1), since a step on black reads smaller. Body on a surface 8.11:1, titles 13.33:1. (Changed on 2026-10-30 from #1a1a1a, which left the bars flat on the black page.)
- #2a2a2a: the hairline edge of a surface and the bar separator only.
- #252525: a pressed or hovered button. Secondary text on it 4.55:1. (Moved from #242424 with the surface on 2026-10-30, so it still shows on one; #282828 was tried and left: secondary text on it fell to 4.38:1.)
- #3a3a38: text selection. Body on it 5.55:1, titles 9.12:1.

### Not defined yet
- Further palettes (sepia, a warm night) — later, as named themes.

## Type
- **Literata** (TypeTogether, OFL) for titles, body and labels: titles in a light display optical size, body in the text size.
- **EB Garamond italic** (Georg Duffner, OFL) for pull quotes only: a centred paragraph, larger than the text (1.36em, weight 400). Literata's italic is too upright to read as a quote at that size. Chosen on 2026-10-26 over Newsreader italic: it reads as italic at once, holds up on the night page, and gives the one bookish moment on an otherwise modern page.
- Nothing else: no sans, no mono for now.
- Both OFL: bundled in the app, self-hosted, default UI — covered.
- Literata was chosen because it was made for long reading on screens.
- **Body:** Literata at weight 360, 18px on the phone's page and 16px on the desktop's, line height 1.55, old-style figures; paragraphs spaced by one line, no indent. Sizes here are the page's own, before the screen scale (see Layout, The page). Settings change these within fixed steps (see Control).
- **Title:** weight 300, optical size 60, 2.05 × the body size, line height 1.12, slightly tightened (−0.012em). Title only — no label above it naming the kind of piece (essay, journal) in v1.
- **Running head:** from page 2 on, the piece title (the chapter in a book, the date in a journal) in small italic (13px), in secondary grey, like a book's running head: centred over the text column, whatever sits left and right of it (changed on 2026-10-30: centred between the head's controls, it sat off the column when they differed in width). On a phone it carries a small ⌄ and opens Contents (see Control).
- **The folio:** the page number, centred under the text column in the page's foot margin, 13px, secondary grey, lining and tabular — as in a printed book (moved out of the page button on 2026-10-30). On a phone it sits level with the page button. Not on a title page, not in the journal (the day's words stand there instead), and not while the tools bar or the keyboard is up, when the bar's count says the page.
- **Drop cap (optional):** the first letter of the piece, four lines deep, weight 600 (SemiBold), optical size 72, title colour, upright. (Three lines read too small, and at weight 500 the hairlines were light beside the text; both changed on 2026-10-27. An EB Garamond italic cap was tried and left: it broke the opening.) Where a browser lacks `initial-letter`, a floated letter of the same depth stands in.
- **Pull quote:** one paragraph, centred, with space above and below; no author line in v1.
- **Indented paragraphs (optional, off by default):** as in a printed book, each paragraph's first line steps in by 1.5em and the space between paragraphs goes. The first paragraph of a piece or chapter, and the one after a pull quote, start flush.
- **Italic in the text:** EB Garamond italic, as the pull quote — scaled to 1.17em so its x-height matches Literata's, with line-height 1 so it sits on the same baseline and never moves the leading. Bold in the text is Literata at 600.
- **Numbers in controls** (the folio, the word count) are lining and tabular, so they do not jump.
- **Thousands take a dot, European style:** 1.240 words, never 1,240.

## Control
- No filled button on the page, and no text in letter-spaced capitals. On a phone the tools appear only when summoned (PROJECT.md §4.1) and on selection, and leave when dismissed. On desktop they sit quietly in the head, unraised, and stay while you type (see Layout, Desktop).
- **The page button (phone):** a 44px circle at the bottom right, 16px from the edge, on a surface with a soft lift, showing an asterisk: Literata's own, outlined as an icon (12px wide, in the title colour) so it never depends on the font, and centred by its drawn shape — the glyph sits high on its line. Its label for assistive tech says "Page N of M. Open tools". The page number is the folio at the foot (see Type). (Changed on 2026-10-30 from a face showing the page number, which read as a page indicator, not an action. The line "Your tools are here", shown once beside it since 2026-10-28 to explain the number, is gone with it.)
- **The tools bar (phone):** the page button opens into one rounded bar, in place: "3 of 12 · 1.240 words" in secondary grey, then icon buttons — export, Time Machine, text settings (Aa), paper / night (moon or sun), close. The rarest sits furthest from where the bar opens, so the bar's old order stays where the thumb already knows it. Escape or close folds it back into the button. The bar never comes closer than 16px to the screen's edges: when a long count would push it there on a phone, the count drops its last word — "148 of 312 · 41.240" — and its label for assistive tech still says it in full. (Export added on 2026-10-28. Measured: 333px with "1 of 5 · 1.040 words"; 363px with "148 of 312 · 41.240 words", too wide even for a 390px phone; 325px without the word.)
- **Icons:** one set, drawn for this app: 20px, 1.5px stroke, round caps and joins, all the same colour, none highlighted. Icons that stay on screen — the desktop head's tools, the rail toggle, the library's view switch, paper / night and settings — rest in secondary grey (5.09:1 on paper, 5.73:1 at night) and take the title colour on hover, when pressed, and while the sheet they opened is open, so the text keeps the attention. Icons in a bar you summoned (the tools bar, the writing bar, the selection menu) are in the title colour. (Chosen on 2026-10-30: in the title colour, the head's icons drew the eye away from the page, most of all at night.) Icons sit by their drawn centre, not their box: next to a line of text they centre between its capitals and its lowercase (the rail toggle sits 1.5px lower than its box would put it). Icon buttons are 36px circles with a soft round pressed tint, no border — every round control is fully round.
- **Text settings:** a sheet over the page, applied live as you change it. Each setting is a segmented control, one row each: size (S / M / L: 16 / 18 / 20px on the phone's page, 14 / 16 / 18px on the desktop's), line spacing (tight 1.42 / normal 1.55 / loose 1.72), alignment (left / justified — icons), theme (Paper / Night, with a small swatch), measure (narrow / wide: the page's side margins, halved with wide, so wide gives a longer line on the same page). Drop cap is one on/off switch; Indent paragraphs is another, off by default. "Done" closes it as a text link.
- **Settings belong to the piece:** everything but the theme is saved with the piece, not the device — the writer typesetting their book. Changing one lays the piece out again, so its page count changes. The theme (and night dimming) stays per device: it moves no words. (Chosen on 2026-10-30, with the fixed page.)
- Justified text always hyphenates.
- **Selection menu:** the same rounded bar, floating over the selected words: bold, italic, pull quote, § (start chapter here), and "keep this moment" (a Time Machine save).
- **Writing on a phone:** above the keyboard, the same bar: undo, redo, a separator, then bold, italic, pull quote, § (new chapter), hide keyboard. A phone has no reliable undo of its own, and moments are up to 5 minutes apart, so undo is on the bar. On desktop it stays ⌘/Ctrl + Z and ⌘/Ctrl + Shift + Z, with nothing on screen.
- **Keyboard up:** the page keeps its size. When the caret would go under the keyboard, the page slides up so the caret line sits just above the writing bar. § and the running head stay, on a strip of page colour with a fade of about 104px under it (solid for its first 15%, then to nothing), in the page colour itself, so text leaving the top thins out softly instead of meeting them; once the title has slid away, the running head carries it. This is the one fade on the page, only while the keyboard pushes it. Page numbers do not change when the keyboard comes up. (The fade lengthened on 2026-10-30 from 44px, which cut the text off too quickly. Built best as a mask on the text, so it always matches the theme; a blur was tried and left: it read as frosted glass and left a hard edge.)
- **Moving around with the keyboard up:** drag the text up or down to see the part of the page behind the keyboard (the caret stays where it is); swipe sideways to turn pages; tap a word to move the caret there. "Hide keyboard" in the writing bar puts the keyboard down; return only ever makes a new paragraph. To go far — the start of the piece, another chapter, another day — tap the running head (Contents or Days drops under it), or the page button beside the writing bar, whose count opens them too; all without leaving the keyboard.
- **The scroll mark:** with the keyboard up, a short track at the right edge says the page goes on behind the keyboard or above the head, and where you are in it. 3 × 70, fully rounded, centred in the right margin (10.5px in; 4.5px with the wide measure) and in the height above the keyboard; the thumb is half the track and moves with the drag. Track in the hairline colour (#e2e2de / #2a2a2a, a mark only), thumb in secondary (#676767, 5.09:1 on the paper page; #8c8c8a, 5.73:1 at night). A sign, never a control: it is not pressed or dragged, the text is. Only while the keyboard is up; where the browser cannot move it without script, its own indicator stays instead. (Chosen on 2026-10-31: dragging to see behind the keyboard had no sign it was possible. The thumb in the title colour was left: louder than the icons at rest.)
- **§ is the chapter mark.** Set in Literata like B and I, it starts a new chapter in the writing bar and "starts chapter here" on a selection. Typing `#` at the start of a line does the same.
- **Back to the library:** §, the app's mark, in Literata Light, at the left of the running-head line in a 44px tap area — on a phone 24px in, the text's left edge (the journal's text starts further in; § does not follow it); its label for assistive tech is "Library". On a phone it stands alone (22px). On desktop it opens the head's path: "§ / The harbour in winter" — § (20px), a slash in secondary grey, the piece's name (15px, text colour), then the rail toggle. A long path wraps rather than cutting: the words sit in a block at most 224px wide, so the path never leaves the rail, each slash stays with the word after it, and § and the toggle stay level with the first line. Every glyph on that line is centred on the same ink line as the tools, not on its box: § sits low in its box and is raised to match. (Chosen on 2026-10-30 over the word *Library* in an outline, which sat in a far corner on desktop, and over the app's name, which says nothing while you write. The piece's name now shows on its own pages.)
- **The path (phone):** where a view sits inside another, the head reads as a path: § (home), a slash in secondary grey, then the place you came from in the text colour, 14px — "§ / Settings" in a settings area. The slash has 8px on each side, on desktop too, centred between § and the word; § keeps its 44px tap area round it. Each part takes you back to that place; the view's own title, below, says where you are, so the path never repeats it. § is raised so its ink centres on the capitals beside it. (Chosen on 2026-10-30 over *Settings* in an outlined word button, which read as an action, not a way back.)
- **The running head opens where you are going (phone):** the running head carries a small ⌄ (secondary grey) and is a control: tapped, it takes the pressed tint and drops Contents (a book), Days (the journal) or the pages (an essay) as a sheet under the head. § stays home and the asterisk stays the tools, so each thing at the head does one job. A page with no running head — page 1, a title page — reaches the same sheet through the page button and the count. (Chosen on 2026-10-30 over a drawer from § like the desktop rail, which made home two taps away, and over the tools alone, which hid the chapters behind two taps.)
- **The word button:** a word in a hairline pill — 13px in the title colour, 12px either side, 30px tall inside a 44px-tall tap area, fully round, a 1px outline in the title colour (14.83:1 on paper, 15.44:1 at night), no fill and no shadow. The outline says it can be pressed. Hovered or pressed, it takes the pressed tint. The outline, not the word, lines up with its edge: on desktop the rail's right edge. A word lined up instead left the outline hanging in the margin, against nothing. It is for a word that starts something: *Write* on the desktop rail. Navigation back to a place is a path, not a word button (see The path). Words inside a sentence or at the foot of a sheet (*Undo*, *Export*, *Done*) stay plain words. (Chosen on 2026-10-28: the bare grey word read as a label, not something to press. A surface with a lift was left, so the page button stays the only raised mark. The word in the title colour was tried and left: it outweighed the title. Changed on 2026-10-30 to the title colour after all: *Write* is the library's one primary action, and in grey it read as one more quiet control beside the rail's places; next to the rail it has no page title to outweigh. Its outline went to the title colour with it, the same day: a dark word in a pale outline read as half a button. The word-button outline colours, #c9c9c4 and #3a3a38, went with them.)
- **Focus:** a 2px ring in the title colour, offset 2px, on every control, visible on keyboard focus only.
- **Tooltips:** rare — only where a word's consequence is not visible, as *Put away* (see The library). A small surface, 12px corners, 13px in the title colour, above the control after a short pause on hover or keyboard focus; desktop only. Never the only place the words live: they are the control's description for assistive tech, and on touch the line after the action says them. Icon buttons do not get tooltips for their names.

## Layout
- One page, one narrow column, generous leading. The page carries the design; nothing frames it — no hairline, no fade, no page dots at the foot. (The journal's left margin rule is the one exception; see The journal.)
- Page 1 opens with the piece title like a chapter, then the text. Later pages have the running head instead.
- **The page:** two pages, one for each kind of screen, each designed to read well there. **The phone's page** (screens under 700px wide), 390 wide: the whole screen's width, 18px type at M, about 38 characters a line; its text takes all the height between the head and the foot, so a taller phone holds more lines (24 on a 390 × 826 page). **The desktop's page**, 540 × 834, a paperback page in the shape of a 5.5 × 8.5 in trade paperback: 16px type at M, about 59 characters a line (11 words) and 27 lines, some 300 words, as a printed paperback. The desktop's page keeps its size on every desktop, so the screen never changes a break there; only the text and the piece's settings do. On a phone the screen's height sets how many lines a page holds, so two phones (or Safari and the Home Screen app) can break a piece on different words; the keyboard never changes a break. A piece has one page count on phones and another on desktops — more pages on a phone, as the phone's page holds fewer words. (Chosen on 2026-10-30 over a page sized by the screen, whose breaks moved with every window, keyboard and rotation; and over one page for both, which set a phone's type at 11–13px or left desktop lines at 38 characters. Readability on each screen comes before one count everywhere. Changed on 2026-10-30 for the phone: its fixed page, fitted to the height Safari leaves, was scaled to 0.77 on a 402px phone, so the text sat 70px in from each side at 14px; a phone's page should fill its width and keep its type at reading size.)
- **The screen scale:** one factor, the largest at which the screen's page fits it; the page sits centred. On a phone only the width sets it: the page fills the screen's width, at most 1.3. It is a CSS size factor, not a transform: every size on the page — the type, the margins, the page's width and height — is written as its page size times the factor, so the browser lays the page out at the screen's size directly and every line still ends on the same word. What sits around the page is not scaled: the space between the page and the screen's edges, and the controls (§, the page button, the rail, the head's tools, the bars and sheets), keep their own sizes and distances at the screen's edges. Print sets its own factor for the paper and keeps the same pages. A 390px phone shows its page at 1, a 402px phone at 1.03 (M at about 18.6px), an iPhone SE (375px) at 0.96. On desktop never more than 1.3: a 1440 × 800 laptop sets M at about 15.3px, a 2000 × 1020 window at about 19.5px, a 1080p screen at about 20.7px, and past that a big screen shows more room around the page, not bigger type. The desktop's page never shrinks past 0.9, so M stays at 14.4px at the least; the phone's page always has at least 502px of text between the head and the foot. A window too small for either (under 658px tall on a phone, 765px on desktop, or 320px wide) scrolls instead. (Added on 2026-10-30: the type kept shrinking as a window got shorter.) **In a window smaller than the page,** the page scrolls as one: the head stays at the top, and once the page has scrolled it stands on a strip of page colour with the same 58px fade under it as One head, so text thins out before it meets the head; the folio is part of the page and scrolls with it, at its foot, never over the text; the page button floats, as it does over the library. The library and settings never make the window scroll: they fill it and scroll themselves, so there is one scrollbar, not two. (Decided on 2026-10-30: the head and the folio stayed at the window's edges while the text scrolled under and over them.) Nothing frames the page: its edge is not drawn.
- Margins, on the page: on the phone's, 64px above the text, 92px below, 24px each side; on the desktop's, 64px above, 84px below, 36px each side. The wide measure halves the side margins. With the keyboard up, the page keeps its size and slides up (see Control).
- **One head on every screen:** the library, a page and settings share one head line, so moving between them nothing jumps. § is always in the same place at the same size — on a phone 24px in, on the line from 10 to 54px; on desktop 20px in, centred at 35px — with the place's name beside it in the same type: the piece's name on a page, *Settings* in settings, *Elbowpatch* (desktop) or *Library* (phone, once scrolled) in the library. The screen's actions sit at the head's right, never on a title's line or inside the content. On a phone the head never scrolls away: when a list scrolls, the head stays on a strip of page colour with a fade 58px tall under it (solid to nothing, in the page colour), the icons stay where they were, and once the big title has gone its place shows small at the left, as a path — "§ Library", as "§ / Settings" in a settings area. (Chosen on 2026-10-30: the head sat at a different height, size and place on each screen, so changing screens jumped, and on a phone it scrolled away with the list, which read as a web page, not an app. No colour changes.)
- **On a phone:** § at the top left, 24px in, the running head centred, the page button at the bottom right 16px from the edge, the folio centred at the foot. That is all the page carries besides the text.
- **On desktop: a rail and a quiet head.** (Chosen on 2026-10-30, replacing two controls in opposite corners of the screen, which left a wide screen feeling like a phone layout in the middle of a desktop: you had to know the corners were there.)
  - **The rail:** a column 336px wide at the left of the window, in page colour, with no surface and no shadow. It is the same width in the library and on every page, so nothing jumps between them, and it never pushes the text: the text column stays centred on the window, open rail or not. Its edge is a hairline in the surface line, the same line as the journal's margin rule — #e2e2de on paper (1.17:1), #2a2a2a at night (1.34:1). It is decorative, never the only sign of anything: the rail reads as a rail without it. The journal draws no edge, since its margin rule already is its line. (Chosen on 2026-10-30 at 1.07:1 after a visible line felt like a frame; changed the same day to the journal's line, as the faint one was too hard to see and the app now has one hairline colour.)
  - **The head:** one line, centred at 35px from the top, on every screen: the path (§ / piece name / rail toggle) at the left, 20px in; the running head centred over the text column; the tools at the right, 20px in — the count ("18 of 48 · 9.420 words") and the icon buttons export, Time Machine, Aa, paper / night, unraised. Paper / night is the last, in the corner, on every screen that has it. Under the head, the rail's first line and the right column's first line start 76px from the top, and a rail row and its pressed tint are 296px wide, on every screen. Rows stand 4px apart, so two tints — the open row and one hovered — never touch; a spacer that was there keeps what is not a row where it was, so the places still start at 76px. (Added on 2026-10-30: touching tints merged into one block.) Clicking the count does nothing on desktop: Contents and Days are in the rail. Sheets (text settings, export) drop from the head under the control that opened it, right-aligned to it, and that control keeps the pressed tint while its sheet is open.
  - **While you type** the head stays as it is: the path, the tools, the running head and an open rail. (A fade of the path and the tools on the first key was built on 2026-10-30 and taken out the same day as not needed: the head is already quiet, and it kept disappearing while writing.)
  - **On a page the rail is the piece:** a book's chapters with the page each starts on, the current one with the pressed tint, then *+ New chapter*; an essay's pages by their first words; the journal's days — *Today* first ("nothing yet" until begun), then each written day with its words, under its month. It starts under the head's line with one line in secondary ("6 chapters · 48 pages"), since the path already names the piece. The rail toggle (an icon: a sheet with a column at its left) folds it away and opens it again; folded, only the path stays, in the same place. Open or folded is remembered per device. On desktop this replaces the Contents and Days sheets.
  - The controls stay within a **frame at most 2560px wide, centred on the screen**: up to an iMac 27" (2560 points) they sit 20px from the screen's edges; on anything wider they stay within the frame. (Widened on 2026-10-28 from 1280px.)

## The library
- Where the app opens.
- **On a phone:** "§ Library" set like a piece title — the place's name with the app's mark in front, the same § that brings you home from a page. At the right of the head line, three quiet icon buttons, in secondary grey: the view switch, paper / night, then settings — on the head's line, where § sits on every other screen, not on the title's. The title starts 64px from the top of the screen, the same as the page's top margin: the status bar covers 47–59px of that on a current iPhone, so it is clearance, not empty space. The list starts 14px under the title's line. (The § added on 2026-10-30; the space under the title cut from 29px to 14px the same day, where the space was really empty.)
- **On desktop: the library rail** (see Layout, Desktop). On its top line — the head's line — § Elbowpatch, set as the path on a page (§ 20px, *Elbowpatch* 15px in the text colour), and *Write* at the rail's right edge. Under it the places — *Library* (the pressed tint when open), *Books*, *Journal*, *Essays*, each with its count at the right in secondary — and at the rail's foot *Put away* and *Settings*. A place opens that kind's pieces alone. A kind with nothing in it stays in the rail, its word and its 0 in secondary grey, so the library always shows what it can hold. There is no big title: the rail says where you are, so the list starts with *Where you left off*. The view switch and paper / night sit at the right of the head, where the tools sit on a page. (Chosen on 2026-10-30: a title floating over a short or empty list read as a word adrift in the window.)

## Books and chapters
- A book is one piece made of chapters. A chapter starts on a fresh page with its title set like page 1's, and no running head on that page — as a printed book does. Later pages carry the chapter title as their running head.
- **The title page:** a book opens on its own title page, as a printed book does: the title centred across the page in the upper third (the optical centre, not the dead centre, where a block looks to sink), clearly larger than a piece title (3 × the body, light), so it stands well apart from the subtitle, and under it an optional **subtitle** in Literata italic, 1.15 × the body, in the text colour. Empty, the title shows "Untitled" and the subtitle "Subtitle", both in secondary grey and always, so it is clear both can be written in. (Changed on 2026-10-28 from showing "Subtitle" only while on the title page: hidden, nobody found it.) The placeholder lies behind the empty line, so the caret stands centred, where the words will start, never at the line's left edge. No author line (no bylines). Chapter 1 begins on page 2. A new book opens with the caret in its title; Return goes on to the subtitle, then chapter 1's title, then the text. No running head on the title page. The subtitle is kept in the outline with the title, so the Time Machine remembers it. (Chosen on 2026-10-28: the book's name had no place on the page, and could only be set from the library; centred with a subtitle on 2026-10-28, after real books' title pages; enlarged from 2.4 × on 2026-10-28, when the title and subtitle read too alike.)
- A new chapter's title shows "Chapter title" in secondary grey until written, then "Begin anywhere."
- The folio keeps counting the whole book.
- **Contents on a phone:** tapping the running head (or "18 of 48" in the tools bar, from a page with no running head) drops a sheet under the head, like text settings: "Contents" and the book's page count, then each chapter with the page it starts on (right, tabular, secondary). The current chapter has the soft pressed tint. "+ New chapter" and "Done" are text links at its foot. (+ since 2026-10-30: § is home at the top left and the chapter break inside the text; as a list action it repeated the home mark.)
- **Contents on desktop** is the rail (see Layout, Desktop), with *+ New chapter* under the chapters.
- On either, Contents is never a page in the book, so page numbers do not shift while writing; on export a contents page is printed at the front.

## The journal
- A journal is one piece; each day begins on a fresh page with the date as its title ("Monday 26 October"). The date is not editable — an entry with its own title is an essay.
- **The title page:** a journal opens on a title page as a book does, before its first day: its own name ("Journal", "Dream journal") and an optional subtitle, centred in the upper third, set as a book's. A new journal is called "Journal", selected, so typing names it; Return goes on to the subtitle, then to the first day's text. The name can also be changed from the library. (Changed on 2026-10-28: the name could only be set from the library, so a new journal seemed to have no title.)
- Opening the journal lands on today. Turning back goes to earlier days. A long day runs onto the next page, with the date as the running head.
- **The margin:** the journal alone has a hairline (the surface-line colour) down the left margin, from top to bottom; on a phone it starts under the head, so § (24px in, as on every screen) sits at its top end. It sits 20px left of the text, which starts further in to make room (44px into the phone's page, 56px into the desktop's), scaled with the page like every other size on it. A deliberate exception to "nothing frames the page", for the journal only. On desktop the rail draws no edge beside the journal, so the page shows this one line, not two.
- **The day's words** sit at the foot, upright, 12px secondary: "312 words" at the text's left edge, level with the page button on the right on a phone. The journal has no folio: a day is found by its date, not a page. Not rotated, not beside the title. (Chosen on 2026-10-27 over a count beside the title, which sat at no clear line.)
- **On desktop** the days are in the rail: *Today* first, then each written day with its words, under its month (see Layout, Desktop).
- **The way in on a phone:** the running head (the date, from a day's page 2 on) or "26 Oct · 312 words" in the tools bar opens a Days sheet as a **month** (in the MVP; changed on 2026-10-27 from a list): the month and year between two arrow icon buttons for the months before and after, a seven-column grid under one-letter weekday initials (12px, secondary), each day a round button — written days in the title colour at weight 560, today with the pressed tint and a hairline ring, days still to come disabled. At the foot, "19 days written in October" (13px, secondary) and "Done". Each day is labelled for assistive tech ("26 October, written").
- The same sheet is also drawn as a list in the Figma file, as an alternative not in the MVP: "Days" and how many are written, the month in italic, then each written day with its word count, today first ("nothing yet" until begun).

## The Time Machine
Designed on 2026-10-27. How it opens and what one version holds are in docs/decisions/0008 and 0005.
- **One moment, the whole piece:** the Time Machine stands at a point in time and shows the whole piece as it was then. Turning pages crosses chapters (or days) as reading does; a chapter that did not exist then is not there. The tape steps through the moments when the section on screen changed, so every step shows a difference; turning into another section keeps the same time and shows that section as it was then.
- **The way in:** the Time Machine icon in the tools (the head on desktop, the tools bar on a phone), or ⌘/Ctrl + Alt + Z (a step further back than undo). It takes the place of the page and opens on the most recent moment that differs from now, so the first thing seen is already the past.
- **The past reads by its frame, never by its text:** the old text keeps full contrast — no tint, no grey, no caret, and night does not dim earlier paragraphs. The head stays the page's own: § goes home, and the running head with ⌄ opens Contents (or Days, or the pages) as it was — in the Time Machine the running head shows on every page, page 1 included, so that sheet is always one tap away. Everything about the moment lives in the moment bar, which takes the foot in place of the page button and the folio; ✕ at its top right ("Back to now") goes back to the present, as does Escape. On desktop the path goes one level deeper — "§ / The harbour in winter / *Sunday evening*", the moment in italic — and the book's name goes back to now; the rail shows Contents as it was, and the tools leave the head. (Chosen on 2026-10-30 over *Now* and *Contents* as outlined words in the corners, which read as actions, and over "§ / Now" as a path, where *Now* read oddly as a place.)
- **The head:** the running head does its usual job — the chapter title in a book, the date in a journal, the essay's title — and opens Contents or Days **as it was at that moment**, dropping under the head as on a page. Navigation sits at the top; what you can do with the moment, and the way out of it, sit at the foot.
- **The moment bar:** one rounded surface, like the tools bar. Full width less 16px each side on a phone; centred at the column's width on desktop. Three parts, top to bottom:
  - **The moment**, named in plain words, Literata italic 15px, title colour: "Sunday evening".
  - **The tape:** one 1px tick per moment, evenly spaced (about 11px; up to 44px when there are few, so the tape spreads across the bar), sliding under a fixed 1.5px hairline in the title colour that marks this moment. The right end is now; the tape is as long as the history, and older moments come in from the left. Kept moments ("keep this moment") are ticks in the title colour; the rest are secondary. Labels (12px, secondary) sit under taller ticks and change unit with age, as thinning does: hours today ("9:00", "13:00", "Now"), weekdays within the week ("Sat", "Mon"), dates within the year ("16 Oct"), months before ("Sep"). A label that would meet its neighbour is left out, as on a ruler. The tape fades at its edges to show it goes on; labels never fade.
  - **The actions:** two equal halves, 44px tall, split by the bar separator: *Bring … back* and *Keep a copy*, in the title colour.
  - The tape is dragged or swiped itself, glides and settles on a tick; a tap on a tick goes to it. No arrows.
- **Bring … back** names what it replaces: *Bring chapter back* in a book, *Bring day back* in a journal, *Bring essay back* in an essay. Nothing else in the piece changes. It does not ask first, because nothing is lost: it closes onto the page with the old text in place, and a calm line shows at the foot for a few seconds (two lines on a phone) — "Chapter 3 brought back from Sunday evening. The rest of the book is as it was." The text it replaced stays a moment, never thinned.
- **Removed and renamed chapters:** Contents as it was lists the chapters of that moment under their titles of that moment; a chapter removed since carries "removed since" in secondary where its page number would be. It opens like any other, and *Bring chapter back* puts it back in its old place — or at the end, if the chapters around it are gone — with its old title. Contents as it was has no "+ New chapter": the past is read-only.
- **Bringing back a part:** the old text is read-only but selectable. A selection shows a bar with two words: *Bring part back* and *Copy*. Bringing a part back returns to now and inserts the passage where it stood — after the paragraph that came before it, found as a reading place is (paragraph, offset, a few words), or at the end of the section if that paragraph is gone. It arrives as its own paragraph, left selected so it can be moved or trimmed, and one undo removes it. It never replaces or merges anything. Its line: "Brought back from Sunday evening."
- **Keep a copy** copies the section only and opens the copy at once as a new essay ("Chapter 3, draft 2"), with one line: "A copy of Sunday evening, kept as a new essay." The piece it came from is untouched. Copying a whole book as it was is after the MVP.
- **Nothing earlier:** the bar holds one line, "Nothing earlier yet. Moments are kept as you write." — no tape, no actions.
- **Dates in plain words:** "Just now" under a minute; "12 minutes ago" within the hour; "This afternoon, 14:05" today; "Yesterday evening, 21:10"; "Sunday evening" within the week; "14 October" this year; "14 October 2025" before. A 24-hour clock. Morning 5–12, afternoon 12–18, evening 18–23, night 23–5.
- **Moving around:** sideways swipe on the page, Page Up / Page Down, Alt + ← / → and the side margins still turn pages of the old text; swipes on the tape move the tape. The tape is a slider: ← / → step a moment when it has focus, Home goes to the oldest, End to now.
- **For assistive tech:** the tape is a slider whose value is the full date ("Sunday 25 October, 21:10"); a change of moment is announced, as a page turn is.

## Export
Designed on 2026-10-28. What the files hold is in docs/decisions/0007.
- **Two ways in, one sheet:** the export icon in the tools (the head on desktop, the tools bar on a phone), and *Export* in the piece menu in the library. The icon is a sheet with a short arrow coming down out of it — taking a copy out — never a box with an arrow going up, which promises sharing.
- **The export sheet:** a sheet like text settings: "Export", with the piece's title at the right in secondary, as Contents shows its page count, then two rows, each a word in the title colour with one line under it in secondary:
  - *Markdown* — "One .md file, plain text any app can open." It downloads at once as `<Title>.md`: the whole piece in one file, a book with a `# ` heading per chapter, a journal with a `# ` heading per day that carries the year (`# Monday 26 October 2026`, though the page shows the date without it) so the file can be read back across years; no history. The sheet closes, and one calm line shows at the foot of the screen for a few seconds — on the page as the *Bring … back* line does, in the library on its small bar (see The library): "Saved as The Long Winter.md."
  - *PDF* — "Numbered pages to print or keep, with contents at the front." for a book; "…, each day on a fresh page." for a journal; "Numbered pages to print or keep." for an essay. It opens the browser's print dialog.
  - "Done" as a text link at its foot.
- **The printed page reads like the piece, not the app:** always paper colours, whatever the theme. The paper size is the print dialog's. The margins are set by the text, not the paper: the column keeps its reading measure — about 65 characters, 117mm at 11pt — centred on the sheet, so the margins are what is left: about 46mm at the sides on A4, 49mm on Letter, never less than 20mm; 25mm above, and 30mm below, where the page number sits. Type in points: body Literata 11 with the screen's leading (1.55) and paragraph space; titles 2.05 × the body, light, as on screen; the running head 8.5 italic, centred in the top margin; the page number 9, centred in the foot margin. Page 1 opens with the title as on screen, with the drop cap and indented paragraphs if the piece uses them; the running head carries from page 2; each chapter and each day starts on a fresh page, as on screen. The page number in secondary grey, lining figures, centred — the folio, as on screen. A book opens with a contents page that is neither numbered nor counted, so the first chapter starts on page 1, as in a printed book: the book's title set as a title, then *Contents* in small italic secondary, then each chapter with its printed page number (right, tabular, secondary). A journal has no contents page; its margin rule prints, 12mm left of the text, and its page number sits flush with the text's right edge instead of centred — the rule holds the left, the number the right, as the page button does on a phone; centred, it floated against the rule. (Chosen on 2026-10-28 from pages drawn on A4 and Letter in the Figma file.) Nothing of the app is printed: no *Library*, no page button, no mark.
- **When a save fails:** "Can't save right now. Your words are still here." and *Save a copy* — one calm line at the foot of the page, where the *Bring … back* line shows, in the same style. Unlike that line it stays until a save succeeds, then goes. *Save a copy* downloads `<Title>.md`, the same file as *Markdown*. The line is announced to screen readers.

## Patterns
- Patterns to follow: settings as a quiet sheet over the page, applied live; on a phone, a single round control that opens into a bar; on desktop, a rail and a quiet head; a path (§ / where you came from) for the way back, never an outlined word; themes named for a mood, not a hex; writing tools on the selection, not in a toolbar; a calm one-line serif sentence for empty and closing states.

## Behaviour
- **Paging, not scrolling.** Writing fills a page; the next page begins when it is full and the view turns to it. Page breaks re-flow as text changes.
- **Turning a page** (decided 2026-10-27):
  - **Phone:** swipe sideways — left for the next page, right for the one before. No tap zones at the edges: a tap in the text places the caret, and the 24px side margin is too narrow to tell the two apart. A vertical drag is kept for seeing what is behind the keyboard (see Control).
  - **Desktop:** Page Up / Page Down or Alt + ← / →, and a click in the wide side margins turns back (left) or forward (right), but only farther than 80px from the text, where no click is aimed at a word, and never at the end of a drag, which is selecting. There the pointer becomes an arrow drawn as our icons are (← or →, 1.5 strokes, round ends, in the title colour with a page-coloured edge), only where there is a page to go to, and it holds while the button is down, so clicking through many pages never flickers it. The margins are for turning: on the first or last page a click there does nothing — it never reaches the text, and the caret stays where it was. (The band beside the text added on 2026-10-28; the arrows, and margin clicks kept from the text, on 2026-10-28.)
  - **Scrolling does not turn pages,** with the wheel or the trackpad, up, down or sideways. The page is not a scroll, and a scroll that turned one page and no more was neither paging nor scrolling, only confusing; a sideways swipe is also the browser's Back, so on the page it does nothing at all. (Decided on 2026-10-28, over one page per gesture.)
  - Each turn is the same quiet slide, and is announced to screen readers.
  - **A turn takes the caret along**, to the end of the text on the page turned to, forward or back: that is where words are added, almost never in between, so typing goes straight on without a click. While writing always; on desktop also while reading, so a turn always leaves the page ready to type (not when the tools or a sheet have the focus). On a phone a swipe while reading does not raise the keyboard: a tap places the caret. Where the page ends mid-paragraph the caret sits just before its last character (mostly the space after the last word): the position after it is also the next page's first, and a browser may paint the caret there or at the page's top. On the last page that is the end of the piece. A book's title page, which holds no text, takes it to the end of the book's title. At the first or last page nothing turns, and the caret stays put. (Decided on 2026-10-28, after the caret was left floating where the title is; the end of the page, over its first letter, the same day.)
  - **↑ and ↓ move a line at a time, keeping their place across;** aiming past a shorter line's end, the caret stops after its last word (not after the space where the line wraps, which a browser can paint on the next line). On a page's last line ↓ goes to the next page's first line; on its first line ↑ goes back to the last line of the page before. On the text's first line ↑ goes to its first letter, and on its last line ↓ to its last letter, as in any text field.
  - **← and → cross a page break in one press:** from a page's last letter to the next page's first, and back. Where a page breaks mid-paragraph, the spot after the earlier page's last space is also the next page's first, and a browser can paint the caret there at the page's top (Firefox does): the caret never stops on it, but goes a letter on, the way it was going. (Decided on 2026-10-28, from a recording of the caret painted beside a chapter's title.)
  - **A selection grows across pages with the keys,** as in any text field: Shift with ↑ / ↓ (a line at a time, as above), ← / →, ⌘/Ctrl + ↑ / ↓, and Shift + Page Up / Page Down, which grows it to the end of the page turned to. The page turns once the selection's moving end is on another page, and not while that end still touches the page in view, so it never turns back and forth at a break. Alt + ← / → turns pages only alone: with Shift, ⌘ or Ctrl they move or select by word, as the system does. (Decided on 2026-10-30, after selecting by word turned the page and selecting at a page's edge turned it back and forth.)
- **A page is a fixed page for its kind of screen,** laid out at the current text settings (see Layout, The page). Larger text makes more pages, never a scroll, so nothing slides under the head or the foot (only a window smaller than the page scrolls; see Layout, The screen scale). (Chosen on 2026-10-30 over a page that was whatever fitted the screen, decided on 2026-10-27, whose breaks moved with every window, keyboard and rotation.)
- **Page numbers are for orientation, not addresses.** They follow the kind of screen, as on an e-reader: page 18 on a phone is not page 18 on a desktop, but every desktop agrees. Finding things is by chapter (Contents) and by date (journal), never by page number. Fixed, printable numbers come only from the export.
- **Your place travels with the piece.** The app keeps where you were: the caret if you were writing, otherwise the first line on screen — as a paragraph, an offset and a few words of its text, so it survives edits made elsewhere (if the words are gone, the nearest paragraph). With a connected place this goes with the piece, so another device opens on the page that holds it, caret restored. The most recent position wins; it is only a position, nothing is lost. **Coming back** to a piece left while writing — from the library, or on the next launch — puts the caret where it was, ready to type, on desktop. On a phone the piece opens on that page with the caret placed, and the keyboard waits for a tap: raised unasked, it would cover half the page. A piece left while reading opens on that page, with no caret. (Decided on 2026-10-28.)
- **The turn** is a quiet horizontal slide (timing in Motion). With `prefers-reduced-motion` it is an instant cut. A page change is announced to screen readers; typing is not.
- **Night focus:** paragraphs before the current one dim to #8c8c8a; the current paragraph stays at body colour. Never dimmer than 4.5:1. Paper does not dim.
- **The tools bar** opens out of the page button and folds back into it (phone). On desktop the head's tools stay where they are.
- Settings and the theme are remembered; until the writer picks, the theme follows the system.

## Motion
Proposed, **not yet reviewed** — carried over from the first prototype, where motion was never checked. Decide each before it is built; these are starting points, not rules.
- What moves: the page turn (a horizontal slide), the tools bar opening out of the page button and folding back (phone), the rail folding and opening, the *Write* menu dropping, the library fading in over the page, sheets (text settings, Contents, Days, confirmations) appearing over the page, the night dimming as the caret moves between paragraphs, the theme changing, the page sliding up when the keyboard comes, and the Time Machine opening and changing moment.
- Starting values: the turn 460ms ease-out; the tools bar opening 340ms from the 44px circle; the library fade 220ms; colour changes (theme, night dimming) 300–400ms; presses 160ms; the Time Machine opening and closing as a 220ms cross-fade; a change of moment a 120ms fade in place — never a slide, so it never reads as a page turn; the tape follows the finger, then glides and settles on the nearest tick.
- Fixed already: one quiet kind of motion, never a 3D paper curl; with `prefers-reduced-motion` every move is an instant cut; nothing moves while you type except the page turning when it fills.

## Imagery
- None in the interface. Photos inside a piece are allowed only when cleanly placed and shaped — small, framed, beside or between paragraphs, never full-bleed. Not designed yet: see Gaps.
- **The mark:** § in Literata at display size — the chapter mark already on the page. The app icon is § in paper white on the night black; a paper version (dark § on paper) serves light places. The wordmark is § then "Elbowpatch" in the title's face (Literata Light). Both are in the Figma file. Inside the app the mark appears large only once, in the empty library. Small, it is the way home at the top left of every page (§), the start of every path (§ / Settings), the title of the phone's library (§ Library), the desktop rail's wordmark, and the colophon at the end of a phone's library with pieces in it (see The library). The README uses the paper wordmark card (dark ink on paper, 16px corners), outlined as `docs/brand/wordmark.svg`.

## Gaps
Open — treat as undesigned, not as solved by a familiar pattern:
- The writing state on a real phone: designed (keyboard up, writing bar), but never tested with a real keyboard, or with editing in the middle of a long piece.
- Motion: proposed, not reviewed (see Motion).
- Page counts across devices: a piece has more pages on a phone than on a desktop. Ideas, not decided: keep the count of the device the piece is usually written on; show the desktop's page number on a phone, as e-readers show a print edition's pages; explain it on a help page. And on desktop, two pages side by side as an open book.
- The Time Machine: designed (see The Time Machine), but the tape has not been tried in the hand — its glide and settle, and a history of a year; whether a passage brought back lands in the right place after heavy edits needs a test once the page is built.
- Settings areas not yet drawn: Appearance, About & privacy.
- Help, About and Source from the colophon: where each leads is not designed — a help view in the app or a page outside it, whether *About* opens Settings › About & privacy, and whether Help is also a row in Settings. The help page *Show me how* opens (adding to the Home Screen) does not exist yet. The empty library at night is not drawn with the links.
- Reordering chapters and Collections (folders) are after the MVP.
- Printed page numbers: a browser's own print cannot number the pages of a contents page, and not every browser prints a number at the foot from a stylesheet. The printed page may need paging by the app before printing. Try it in Safari and Chrome before the PDF is promised as designed.
- A photo placed inside a piece.
- The desktop rail and head (2026-10-30), drawn in paper, and at night only for a journal day and an essay: the night hairline (#1e1e1e on #0e0e0e) is unchecked by eye; between 700px and about 1280px wide the rail meets the text column, and whether the rail then folds itself or covers the margin is undecided; keyboard focus order across the path, the rail and the tools is not set.
- Desktop Settings with the rail, the desktop shelf, and the piece menu on desktop are drawn as the simplest analogue of the rail, not reviewed.
- The journal's Days as it was, dropping from the running head in the Time Machine, is not drawn.
- On a phone: the top fade is 105px on paper and 103px at night in the Figma file, to be made one value; whether the running head's ⌄ is found without a hint; a chapter title too long for the line beside §; the *Write* bar opening from the pill; the Contents sheet with the keyboard up.
- Where a line in the library (after putting away or exporting) sits on desktop, now that there is no button at the foot.
- The status bar: the app draws under it (`viewport-fit=cover`) but adds no `env(safe-area-inset-top)`, so the 64px top margins are all that keep text clear of it. The Figma frames do not draw a status bar, so the top looks emptier there than on a phone.
- Installing: an invitation to add the app to the Home Screen (or install it), and an area for it in Settings. What it must say is in docs/decisions/0010; how it looks is not designed.

Paged writing is new ground: typing on a paged page, a phone keyboard, Markdown, an empty first page and a long title being written all still need designing.

## Do not
- Scroll a long page endlessly; page it.
- Fade over readable text, or add a hairline, fade or dots at the foot of the page. (The exceptions: the fade under the head while the keyboard pushes the page up, and under the head of a list, or of a window smaller than the page, once it has scrolled.)
- Use a 3D paper curl, page textures, parchment, typewriter fonts, quills, or any old-writer illustration in the interface.
- Add an accent colour, cream, or amber in this pass.
- Add a third font family without a reason recorded here.
- Label the kind of piece above the title, or credit a pull quote, in v1.
- Draw the desktop rail's edge as a visible line, or give the rail a surface or a shadow.
- Let the rail push or shift the text column: the page stays centred on the window.
- Put a filled button on the page, or reader features (search book, bookmarks, ratings, bylines).
- Use any text colour below 4.5:1 on its page.
- Use Git words in the UI (PROJECT.md §3): say moments, drafts, bring this back.
- Borrow another product's name, typefaces or colours.
